# Composite video: technical notes

## PC Engine composite output (HuC6260)

Sources: Furrtek's decap of the HuC6260 and his electrical test-mode readout of its colour lookup ([furrtek.org](https://www.furrtek.org/?a=pceyuv), [readout program](https://gist.github.com/furrtek/1ae9e023e0a7db030258bb7dfc7d0215/30eac6a9861228f942ccec000c27df86b2c41dfc)), in [Kitrinx's transcription](https://github.com/Kitrinx/TG16_Palette/blob/e8cdf400f970753ccdf5c17446b5b62eead66e0f/bfbii.h) with selected entries checked against Furrtek's photographs; the HuC6260 manual (in this folder; its output-level page is missing from the copy); the PWD-623A (original PC Engine) video schematic reverse-engineered by [Board-Folk](https://github.com/Board-Folk/PC-Engine/blob/70dfd432639dd70ae4d25dc2169c3af27adf632e/video.kicad_sch), rev 1.2.1; the TurboGrafx-16 service manual's base-unit schematic (in this folder); HD Retrovision's tracings of the TurboBooster. No console was measured for this core; the colour fit (item 2) is Kitrinx's, from Artemio Urbina's measurements of a Duo-R.

1. **The HuC6260** (VCE) looks each pixel's 9-bit colour up twice: in its RGB DACs, and in a 512 × 15-bit table that gives 5-bit Y, U and V codes (Furrtek). It runs from a 21.477 MHz clock, six times the colour subcarrier (manual), and outputs Y, R−Y, B−Y and a burst (BURS) on separate pins. The board has no oscillator or modulator, so R−Y and B−Y leave the chip already modulated on the subcarrier (schematic). A line is 1,365 clocks, 227.5 subcarrier cycles, so the carrier phase inverts from one line to the next as in NTSC. Bit 2 of the VCE control register selects 262 or 263 lines per frame: with 262 lines a frame is a whole number of carrier cycles and the chroma pattern stands still; with 263 it inverts every frame. Bit 7 turns the colour off. Composite sync is the horizontal sync inverted during the 3 lines of vertical sync, without equalising pulses (the core's VCE).
2. **The encoder** is the HuC6260 itself; there is no separate encoder chip on any board in the sources. The table gives the colour codes but not the voltages: the manual's output page is not in the copy, so levels are NTSC's nominal ones (white 0.714 V, sync −0.286 V, burst 0.286 Vp-p, black at blanking with no setup pedestal). How the 5-bit chroma codes become voltages is Kitrinx's "double zero" reading (codes 15 and 16 are both zero), with colour strength and hue fitted by Kitrinx to vectorscope measurements of a Duo-R by Artemio Urbina: U and V scales 0.436 and 0.615, V gain 0.96, saturation 192/255, picture phase −10.4°. That fit is the source of the core's existing Original palette. Neither the burst's position nor its phase is documented.
3. **The board** (PWD-623A) sums the pins in resistors: Y through R134 4.7 kΩ, R−Y through R137 6.2 kΩ, B−Y through R138 8.1 kΩ into a node that feeds TR103's base through R156 1 kΩ; BURS joins at the base through R141 27 kΩ. Relative to Y, the weights are 0.758 (R−Y), 0.580 (B−Y) and 0.261 (burst). Three transistors (TR103 2SC945, TR104 2SA733, TR105 2SC945) buffer the sum, and the composite output leaves TR105's emitter through R145 20 Ω to the expansion connector. That tap comes before C117: the parts after it (C117 47 µF, R149 2.7 kΩ, L102, R161 680 Ω, and on the TurboGrafx-16 the FL112 1 nF LC filter) feed only the RF modulator. There is no filter in the composite path. The TurboGrafx-16 board has the same circuit with a few different values (B−Y 8.2 kΩ, PNP base 5.6 kΩ).
4. **The AV booster** (TurboBooster HES-AVB-01/02) adds an emitter follower (R22 1.8 kΩ into Q5 2SC2785, R19 75 Ω to ground) and C16 470 µF into FL3, a common-mode choke, to the RCA jack. Into a 75 Ω set that is a 4.5 Hz high-pass, which the set's line clamp removes.

Not known: the DAC output impedance and settling, the transistors' bandwidth, the carrier's harmonics (the chip may make a square or stepped carrier rather than a sine), the absolute output levels, and the burst's position and phase. These set how soft edges are and some fine-pattern colour artefacts.

**Audio.** The PC Engine board has no filter between the HuC6280's sound pins and the expansion connector; the TurboBooster's audio stage is where the sound is rolled off. Per channel: C8 1 µF into R9 2.7 kΩ at the virtual ground of a c358 op-amp (a 58.9 Hz high-pass), with R15 12 kΩ ∥ C7 1.5 nF in the feedback (an 8.84 kHz low-pass); the 220 pF EMI filters and the 47 µF output capacitor lie far outside the audio band (HD Retrovision's tracing). The base board's RF audio stage is a 75 µs pre-emphasis that the set removes. The CD unit occupies the same connector and has its own outputs, so CD and ADPCM audio never pass through the booster.

## How it is implemented

1. **VCE taps**: the final 9-bit colour, after the VCE's border handling and the SuperGrafx mixer and before the Original palette, plus the VCE's line and frame counters and its frame length (262 or 263). The chain runs on the core's 42.954545 MHz clock, 12 samples per subcarrier cycle, one sample every clock: 2,730 samples per line. Its carrier counter (modulo 12) is never restarted by a line, a frame, a dot-clock change or the greyscale bit, so the carrier keeps the VCE's phase relations in all three dot clocks (5.37, 7.16 and 10.74 MHz).
2. **Encoder** (`huc6260_composite.sv`): the table is a ROM loaded from `huc6260_yuv.hex`. Voltages are signed 24-bit with 21 fractional bits (1.0 = 1 V). Y is 0.714 V / 31 per code; U and V are the codes, read as double zero, times their scale, on 12-entry sine and cosine tables (12 fractional bits) shifted by the −10.4° picture phase. The U and V steps are referred to the pins by the board weights, so that the output carries Kitrinx's fitted amplitudes. Sync replaces Y with −0.286 V. The burst is a sine table at 180° to B−Y, 0.286 Vp-p at the output, for 10 cycles from 0.51 µs after the end of sync (samples 260–379 of the line), and off during vertical sync. With the greyscale bit set, picture chroma is removed and the burst kept. Two register stages.
3. **Board** (`pce_av_board.sv`): Y + 0.758 (R−Y) + 0.580 (B−Y) + 0.261 burst, with 16-bit fractional gains, summed at full width, rounded once and registered. There are no reactive parts in the composite path, so the board is a weighted sum, not a filter; the transistor chain is taken as unity gain.
4. **Composite filters**: Kitrinx's composite decoder from the NES core, run at 12 samples per subcarrier cycle with a 6-sample luma low-pass and one output pixel per 2 samples (21.48 MHz, 1,365 a line, six per subcarrier cycle, so the scaler has enough samples to draw the decoded picture), with the changes listed below. Budget TV uses the notch, Premium TV the adaptive comb. Its contrast input is fixed at 0.714 V to white, saturation at unity, hue at 0 and the chroma tail off. **Comp. Brightness** sets the brightness input to Kitrinx's NES values: Japan 0, USA −53.6 mV (7.5 IRE below blanking), More +35.6 mV, Much More +78.5 mV, Too Much +114 mV. **Comp. Sharpness** sets the luma peaking to 0, 4, 8 or 12 of 15. The visible rows are the VCE's: 19–260 with Overscan Visible, 23–253 with Hidden, in both frame lengths. The picture stays blank after reset until the first vertical sync.
5. **Output**: `pce_cvbs_out.sv` moves each decoded pixel, with its sync and blanking, to the 85.909 MHz video clock as one bundle (a toggle and a two-register synchroniser; pixels are two source clocks apart, and the bundle is taken three video clocks after the toggle, one before the next pixel). The video mixer gets that bundle in place of the native pixels, without the scandoubler, HQ2x or scanlines (the mixer's 640-pixel line buffer is the scandoubler's, so the 1,132-pixel decoded line does not pass through it). Gamma is applied by `gamma_fast` in front of the mixer, because the mixer's own gamma stage needs four clocks per pixel and decoded pixels come every four video clocks with three lookups to make; it has the same curve and the same one-pixel delay, on both paths. The 1080p 216-line crop still applies. In simulation a colour edge leaves the decoder about 30 samples (0.7 µs) after the VCE outputs it.
6. **TurboBooster audio** (`pce_booster_audio.sv`): two one-pole filters per channel at 1.342 MHz (the core clock / 32). The high-pass subtracts a tracked DC level, k = 2⁻¹² + 2⁻¹⁵ + 2⁻²⁰ (58.9 Hz); the low-pass has k = 2⁻⁵ + 2⁻⁷ + 2⁻¹⁰ + 2⁻¹¹ (8.84 kHz). State is 40 bits with 20 fractional bits. The stage's gain of 12k/2.7k is replaced by 49/64, the DC gain of the core's own PSG filter, so the balance with CD audio is unchanged. With PSG Filter on TurboBooster it replaces the core's PSG filter; CD and ADPCM audio are not touched.

Not implemented: DAC settling, transistor bandwidth and carrier harmonics (item "Not known" above), the TurboGrafx-16 board's resistor values, and the AV booster's 4.5 Hz high-pass.

In simulation (Icarus Verilog), the whole chain from VCE taps to decoded pixels passes 51 of 51 checks over three frames of 262 and 263 lines, with both decoders on the same signal: all 512 table entries at all 12 carrier phases, with and without greyscale (12,288 comparisons); the encoder and board within 0.04 mV of a floating-point model; colour bars in all three dot clocks; a carrier that never restarts; constant line width, sync counts and the exact visible rows. The decoder alone passes 59 of 59 checks, and directed tests cover the encoder's gates, control changes, reset and signed rounding. The TurboBooster filter matches its analogue response within 0.01 dB.

## Changes to Kitrinx's composite decoder

The decoder is Kitrinx's from the NES core (MIT licence; the changes are MIT too), in `rtl/composite/composite_decoder.sv`. It started from the Mega Drive core's copy and differs from it where noted.

* **Burst gate and black clamp** are timed from the trailing edge of sync, 1.3 and 3.8 µs after it, as a set's burst gate pulse is, instead of from the leading edge. With a 4.7 µs sync both still fall 6.0 and 8.5 µs after the leading edge, as before; the PC Engine's sync is 4.47 µs.
* **Chroma is blanked** until the back porch is over (3.8 µs after sync ends), as a set blanks it through horizontal blanking, so the burst is never shown.
* **Premium TV comb** uses a set's fixed 227.5-cycle delay instead of one line of the source. A PC Engine line is 227.5 cycles, so the delay is the same. The delay store holds exactly 2,730 samples and wraps, instead of a power-of-two RAM.
* **Sync separator** slices the signal averaged over one subcarrier cycle, as a set's does through its low-pass, instead of the raw signal, so saturated colours cannot dip to the slice level and be taken for sync. Unlike the Mega Drive copy, the average's delay (about 6 samples) is not taken back out, so the picture sits about 1.4 output pixels earlier against the regenerated blanking.
* **Sync tip** starts at the PC Engine's sync level instead of the top of the range, so the first lines after reset are not taken for sync.
* **Horizontal flywheel**: the line counter free-runs at the last accepted line length, and a sync edge restarts it only when it comes within one subcarrier cycle of the expected 2,730 samples. The PC Engine inverts its sync during vertical sync, without equalising pulses, so the separator sees extra edges where vertical sync starts and ends; without the flywheel they give short lines and a horizontal jump. Vertical sync is detected on a separate counter from the raw edges.
* **Frame length and visible rows** come from the core: 262 or 263 lines from the VCE instead of a fixed 262, and the visible rows from the Overscan option instead of a fixed 3 lines before and 17 after vertical sync. The picture stays blank after reset until the first vertical sync.
* **Timing parameters**: the gates, the regenerated sync width, the end of blanking and the front porch are parameters. The core sets the sync width to 192 samples (4.47 µs), the VCE's own; the others keep Kitrinx's values.
* **Samples per cycle** may be odd (the notch's half-cycle delay is then the mean of the two samples around it). This core uses 12.
* Not changing the picture: the sharpness filter's tap spacing is a parameter, the comb's two mixes are folded into one product each and the burst's two squares narrowed to 16 × 16 bits (same results), the comb's store is a RAM inside the decoder rather than the core's dpram, and simulation checks the parameter bounds.

With standard NTSC timing, the decoder gives the same picture as the original, apart from the 1.4-pixel shift against blanking and the visible rows.

## Build

Quartus 17.0.2 Lite, seed 1. Logic 68% (28,370 of 41,910 ALMs), RAM blocks 544 of 553, DSP blocks 90 of 112. Worst setup slack is +0.372 ns and hold slack +0.218 ns, so timing passes.
